Convention used here: forward KL means DKL(p‖q), so q is pushed to cover p. Reverse KL means DKL(q‖p), so q is pushed to stay where p has support. Some authors swap the names, so always check the formula.

Entropy

H(X)=−Σ p(x) log₂p(x)

Average uncertainty, or optimal average bits needed to encode outcomes from P.

Cross-entropy

H(P,Q)=−Σ p(x) log₂q(x)

Average bits used when data comes from P but your code/model assumes Q.

Joint and conditional entropy

H(X,Y)=H(X)+H(Y|X)

Joint uncertainty equals uncertainty in X plus uncertainty left in Y after X is known.

Perplexity

PP(P)=2ᴴ⁽ᴾ⁾

The effective number of equally likely choices. Lower is better only when comparing models on the same data.

KL divergence

DKL(P‖Q)=Σp log₂(p/q)≥0

Extra bits caused by using Q instead of P. It is zero only when P=Q, and it is not symmetric.

KL and maximum likelihood

argmax EP[log Qθ] = argmin KL(P‖Qθ)

Maximum likelihood fits Q by minimizing forward KL from the data distribution to the model.
